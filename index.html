    07 · Methodology

    How the numbers were produced

    • Dataset. OpenML hls4ml_lhc_jets_hlf v1 (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3602260, CC BY 4.0): 830,000 jets, 16 high-level features, 5 classes. Particle-level Monte Carlo; not detector data.
    • Split. 100,000-jet stratified subset; 70/15/15 train/validation/test; seed-controlled.
    • Quantization. Simulated uniform affine (fake) quantization of Linear weights and hidden activations; PTQ calibrated on 20,480 training jets; QAT is 7 fine-tuning epochs at 0.1× learning rate.
    • Selection. No test-set information is used for any choice. The earlier mixed-precision result selected on test data is excluded from every public chart.
    • Statistics. Five seeds; intervals are descriptive t-intervals; no significance test is claimed.

    08 · Reproducibility

    Reproduce from the repository

    • Research results are frozen at commit 970f44a on the research branch.
    • Full instructions, dataset fingerprints and commands: docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md.
    • Two independent runs of the gates and of the architecture experiment produced identical results; the full test suite passes (94 passed, 5 skipped).
    • The CPU/CUDA device caveat for architecture A is documented in the repository and above.

    09 · Research

    Findings and limits

    What the evidence supports

    • INT8 PTQ is practically lossless against FP32.
    • INT6 PTQ costs about 0.001 ROC-AUC.
    • INT4 PTQ degrades substantially in both architectures; INT4 QAT recovers most of the loss.
    • No evaluated mixed-precision layout beat uniform INT6 PTQ under the 3,724 B constraint.

    What it does not support

    • Any claim about detector data, trigger efficiency or physics operating points.
    • Any FPGA resource, latency or physical measurement.
    • Device dependence as an established mechanism (observational only).
    • Generalization beyond two small MLPs on one simulated dataset.
